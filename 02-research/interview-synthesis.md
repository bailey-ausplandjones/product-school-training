# Interview Synthesis — Streakly Comeback

**Source:** 3 user interviews — Priya S. (power user, 14-month streak), Tom R. (churned, left after breaking a 12-day streak at week 5), Amara L. (new user, day 4)
**Context:** Day-7 retention slipped from 48% → 39% after v2 redesign. Target: ≥48%. Prepared for Thursday kickoff.

---

## 1. Top 5 Themes

### Theme 1 — The streak is the whole product (for better and worse)
Every user's story orbits the streak counter. It's what created Priya's ritual, what Tom lost and couldn't get past, and what's already stressing out Amara at day 4.

> "It has become a ritual." — Priya
> "I got to a 12-day streak and I was proud of it." — Tom

### Theme 2 — Losing a streak feels like punishment, not feedback
The "zero" moment is emotionally loaded across the board — not a neutral reset, but a felt loss with no path back.

> "Then I traveled for work, missed two days, and came back to a big fat zero. The app sent me this 'you lost your streak' notification that just made me feel bad." — Tom
> "I don't want to lose everything I've built after four days." — Amara

### Theme 3 — There's a fear/reward tipping point somewhere around day 20–30
Priya's turning point (30-day celebration, ~3 weeks to "stick") sits right where Tom broke (12 days) and Amara now sits (day 4) — suggesting a fragile middle zone where the habit hasn't formed but the loss-aversion pressure has already kicked in.

> "The thing that made it stick was hitting a 30-day streak and the app actually celebrating it... it took me about three weeks to get there." — Priya
> "I think most people quit long before the habit forms." — Priya

### Theme 4 — No recovery mechanism = no second chance
Tom names a concrete, comparable feature gap: competitors let you miss a day without total loss. Streakly's all-or-nothing structure is the direct cause he cites for churning.

> "There was no way to recover it, nothing. So I gave up." — Tom
> "I switched to Duolingo, at least there a missed day doesn't wipe everything, and a streak freeze feels forgiving." — Tom

### Theme 5 — Early enjoyment coexists with early anxiety
New-user experience isn't uniformly bad — the content itself lands — but the streak mechanic is already reframing the product from game to obligation within the first week, before any habit has formed.

> "The first few lessons were genuinely fun." — Amara
> "I check it, but the pressure is starting to feel like a chore instead of a game." — Amara

## 3. Contradictions & Tensions

- **Streak-as-motivator vs. streak-as-punishment, in the same mechanic.** Priya's data point *is* the celebration moment reinforcing the streak — proof the mechanic can motivate. Tom's data point is the exact same mechanic (streak → zero) driving him out. It's not that streaks don't work; it's that the current implementation has no middle state between "milestone celebration" and "total wipeout." This is the core tension named in the working scenario, and these three interviews independently reproduce it.
- **Priya implicitly validates the risk Amara and Tom are naming.** Priya's own comment — "most people quit long before the habit forms" — is a power user acknowledging, unprompted, that the survivorship bias in her own success story is real. She is describing the population that Tom and Amara represent.
- **Only Priya received positive reinforcement; Tom and Amara only describe loss-framing or anticipated loss.** No one mentions a positive milestone before day 30. That's a gap, not a contradiction, but worth flagging: the celebration mechanic Priya credits doesn't appear to exist (or isn't landing) in the first-week experience.

---

## 4. Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

**The product currently has only one emotional register for the streak before day ~30: accumulating pride that can be erased overnight, with no acknowledged middle ground.** Amara is already feeling the *anticipated* version of what happened to Tom — at day 4, before any real habit exists, she's doing loss-aversion math instead of building routine. Tom's churn shows what happens when that fear resolves the way she's afraid of: total, unrecoverable loss, with the app's own notification framing it as a personal failure.

For week-1 retention specifically, this means the fix concept shouldn't only be about *softening the loss* (a freeze, grace period, etc. — real and worth pursuing, but it's a Tom-shaped fix). It should be about *giving new users something other than the streak count to feel proud of before day 7* — because Priya's celebration moment that created lock-in didn't arrive until day 30, and almost nobody in the first week has anything comparable to hold onto. Right now, week 1 asks users to feel invested in something fragile before giving them any reason the fragility is worth the risk.
